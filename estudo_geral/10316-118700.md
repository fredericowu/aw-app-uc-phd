Abstract
T
he increasing complexity of industrial applications demands robust, re-
liable, and deterministic communication solutions, particularly in Low-
Power and Lossy Networks (LLNs), such as those found in the Indus-
trial Internet of Things (IIoT). Time-Slotted Channel Hopping (TSCH), defined
in the IEEE 802.15.4e amendment, is designed to meet these requirements, en-
suring energy eﬀiciency while maintaining strict performance metrics, such as
low latency and high reliability. However, existing TSCH scheduling strategies
often lack the flexibility required to adapt to the dynamic and unpredictable
environments typically encountered in industrial settings.
This research makes several key contributions to the field. First, it presents
a comprehensive survey of assessment metrics for evaluating TSCH schedul-
ing in industrial environments, identifying critical gaps in existing performance
evaluation methods. Second, it pushes the TSCH scheduling mechanisms to
their operational limits, analysing their impact on application by assessing key
performance metrics under high-stress conditions. Third, it introduces the Rip-
pleTrickle scheduling algorithm, which integrates cross-layer information with
the Trickle timer from RPL (Routing Protocol for Low-Power and Lossy Net-
works). This algorithm dynamically adapts to changing network conditions,
optimising communication paths, and enhancing network reliability. RippleT-
rickle Ranked (RT-Ranked) is also proposed, which incorporates the network
topology into the scheduling process, allowing more eﬀicient resource manage-
ment and improving overall network performance.
The proposed solutions were rigorously evaluated at both the application and
Media Access Network (MAC) layers, revealing significant improvements in net-
work stability, latency, energy consumption, and overall eﬀiciency. This thesis
advances the understanding of TSCH scheduling limitations and offers an innov-
ative adaptive scheduling framework tailored for mission-critical industrial ap-
plications that significantly improves network resilience and performance.
Keywords: TSCH, IoT, IIoT, Scheduling

Chapter 1
Introduction
W
e are currently in the midst of the fourth industrial age. The trans-
ition from analogue to digital hardware took place during the third
industrial age. This transition led to interconnected digital devices,
commonly called things, now globally connected through the Internet. The in-
terconnectedness of these devices has led to the emergence of the Internet of
Things (IoT), where they perform various tasks. The IoT has revolutionised
our interaction with the physical world, giving rise to a new business model
that relies on a robust and trustworthy interconnection infrastructure. In the
industrial context, the concepts of IoT have seamlessly integrated into the work
landscape, leveraging its ubiquitous attributes and contributing to the realm of
the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT).
To facilitate the interconnection of IoT devices, academia and industry have
been actively developing new methods and technologies to create increasingly
eﬀicient infrastructures. However, each approach has its unique characteristics
and resource requirements. For instance, the Time-slotted Channel Hopping
(TSCH) and IPv6 over the TSCH mode of IEEE 802.15.4e (6TiSCH) [ 1] have

1.1 Motivation and Problem Statement
As Industry 4.0 continues to evolve, the demand for advanced wireless com-
munication systems capable of supporting the increasing complexity and scale
of industrial operations is growing. Due to the critical nature of their opera-
tions, industrial IoT applications in sectors such as manufacturing, energy, and
automation require highly reliable, real-time communication. Communication
failures or delays can lead to costly downtime or pose significant safety risks.
Thus, deterministic communication protocols, such as TSCH, are essential for
ensuring timely and reliable data transmission.
However, IoT devices used in industrial environments are often resource-
constrained, with limited battery life and computational capacity. Current
TSCH scheduling mechanisms lack the necessary flexibility to cope with dy-
namic and error-prone conditions typical in industrial networks. This rigidity
can result in ineﬀiciencies, particularly when managing varying traﬀic patterns
in unpredictable environments.
In addition, existing network evaluation metrics may not fully capture the unique
challenges posed by TSCH-based networks. Traditional metrics often fail to
consider critical factors such as network stability, latency, and throughput, key
aspects in industrial settings.
Since the TSCH standard does not prescribe a specific scheduling scheme, the
choice of how to allocate and deallocate cells is primarily driven by application
requirements. Current TSCH scheduling heavily relies on application-driven cell
allocation, requiring dynamic adaptation to network conditions based on local
node information. Distributed TSCH schedules use this node-specific inform-
ation to create and maintain precise local schedules. However, the minimum

2.2 Time-Slotted Channel Hopping
The evolution of the IEEE 802.15.4 standard has remained focused on indus-
trial applications [ 36]. Since its first version, the IEEE 802.15 Working Group
for Wireless Personal Area Networks (WPAN) has aimed to create a standard
that enables low-cost and low-power communications. Although 6LoWPAN was
designed to allow the use of IPv6 over IEEE 802.15.4 by constrained devices, its
application in LLNs has introduced new challenges.
In 2012, the 802.15 Task Group 4e (TG4e) introduced an amendment to the
Media Access Control (MAC) protocol, adding five new behaviour modes, with
TSCH being particularly significant. TSCH provides deterministic channel ac-
cess and improves resilience to interference through channel hopping [ 37].
To integrate Internet Protocol (IP) with industrial communication technologies,
the 6TiSCH Working Group (WG) was established. This group focused on cre-
ating an architecture based on open standards, aimed at meeting the industrial
performance requirements of the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF)/IP
suite. The 6TiSCH architecture defines the IPv6 control plane that manages
and orchestrates TSCH networks [ 10].
The TSCH MAC enables nodes to communicate through a 10 ms structure
defined as a timeslot; the timeslot period is enough for transmitting data and
receiving acknowledgement from the recipient in its duration; each timeslot has
an Absolute Slot Number (ASN), which is a 5-byte counter. At network ini-
tialisation, the first timeslot has the ASN value assigned to 0; at each timeslot,
the ASN value is incremented by 1; the TSCH organises these timeslots into
a slotframe, a group of repetitive structures over time. During every slotframe
cycle, the channel number changes according to the channel hopping schema
defined by the standard.

The schedule TSCH was not defined by the standard, but the RFC 7554 [ 11] in-

and advancements. Since then, the focus has been on scheduling techniques.
Therefore, many efforts have been made to elaborate the ideal TSCH Schedul-
ing Function (SF). On top of that, an industry application demands from TSCH
SF a reliable communication balancing among energy, bandwidth and latency.
Hence, the application requirements define the priority of the three cited groups.
E.g., higher bandwidth needs to compromise energy eﬀiciency due to the need
for more communication cells. On the other hand, spending less energy to in-
crease the battery-based network survivability requires a long slotframe length,
impacting latency and bandwidth.

distributed and autonomous. Each TSCH SF method influences the network
formation time, traﬀic capacity, energy consumption, and overhead.
As discussed in Duquennoy et al. [ 41] the slotframe length affects the latency,
the traﬀic capacity and the energy. The major challenge for a TSCH schedule

2.2.2 Scheduling
Scheduling plays a vital role in IIoT networking, and the applications should rely
on a schedule that prevents simultaneously interfering transmissions. The MAC
TSCH specification relies on Time Division Multiple Access (TDMA) and the
channel hopping technique to provide deterministic behaviour with reliability
and energy eﬀiciency.
Since then, work in 6TiSCH has focused on various improvements, and the
scheduling schema has received a lot of attention. The TSCH schedule was not
defined by the standard, but the informational RFC 7554 [ 11] discusses issues
and goals that have been employed as a guideline for research and improvements.
Although RFC 8180 [ 12] defines an IPv6 minimal working mode over TSCH,
the minimal schema is not enough for reaching some critical requirements, as
discussed in [ 19].
As previously discussed, efforts to develop the ideal TSCH SF have resulted
in four scheduling strategies: Static, Neighbor-to-Neighbor, Centralised, and
Hop-by-Hop. However, in the literature, TSCH schedules are often classified
into three types: centralised, distributed, and autonomous. In the centralised
scheduling strategy, as defined by Righetti et al. [ 54], a node acts as the Point
Coordination Element (PCE), responsible for gathering network information,
computing the schedule, and distributing it across the network. In contrast,
Mohamadi and Senouci [ 55] describe distributed approaches as involving nego-
tiation between nodes and their neighbours, resulting in the coordinated schedul-
ing of links between nodes. Rekik et al. [ 56] noted that both centralised and
distributed approaches can incur high overhead.
Autonomous scheduling has been proposed as an alternative to centralised ap-
proaches. According to Elsts et al. [ 57], in an autonomous approach, each node
typically builds its schedule based solely on local information. A well-known
example is Orchestra [ 41], a TSCH scheduler that automatically manages com-
munication schedules based on network events and RPL topology. Orchestra
assigns cells dynamically using hash functions and predefined scheduling rules,
organised into per-slotframe structures for control traﬀic (e.g., Enhanced Beacon
(EB) and RPL messages), unicast transmissions, and shared communication. By
tightly integrating with the RPL routing protocol, Orchestra adapts to changes
in network topology without requiring manual configuration or centralised co-
ordination, aiming to balance energy eﬀiciency, reliability, and scalability.

attributes, and the user might select a TSCH scheduling solution based on these
properties. In Table 2.4, the attributes extracted from the surveys discussed

2.3 IPv6 Routing Protocol for LLN
The RPL routing protocol has become the preferred choice for the IoT and
IIoT applications due to its unparalleled ability to handle unicast and multic-
ast traﬀic. This protocol is especially well-suited for scenarios involving many
sensors and devices. Conceived by the IETF 6LoWPANs WG, RPL [ 58] presents
a versatile and scalable routing solution for low-power networks, making it the
go-to option for IoT and Machine-to-Machine (M2M) communication in indus-
trial automation. It is fundamental in networks using devices with constrained
processing power, memory, and battery life, where it can intelligently optimise
routing paths and conserve energy.
In brief, RPL plays a critical role in ensuring reliable and eﬀicient communication
within IIoT systems. Its adaptable framework allows applications to meet spe-
cific requirements, improving dependability, energy eﬀiciency, and scalability.
By establishing robust communication paths and optimising resource utilisa-
tion, RPL enables IIoT systems to effectively adapt to changing network con-
ditions. As a result, RPL empowers IIoT networks to handle the complexities
and demanding requirements of industrial applications.
RPL provides a mechanism for building and maintaining a Destination-Oriented
Directed Acyclic Graph (DODAG), a directed acyclic graph where all edges are
oriented in the same direction, ensuring no cycles. RPL uses a set of ICMPv6
RPL Control Messages to build and maintain the network’s topology. Figure
2.2 presents the structure of the ICMPv6 RPL Control Message, comprising
an ICMPv6 header (Type, Code, and Checksum), along with the addition of
Base and Options fields. Concerning the last one, option fields in RPL control
messages can carry additional information not included in the basic message
format. The option field can be used to configure the RPL protocol, provide
information about the network topology, or carry application-specific data. The
option field can be used as a powerful tool to extend the functionality of the
RPL protocol.

such as in response to an explicit request. The trickle algorithm [ 59] defines
the periodicity of sending DIO messages. DODAG-attached nodes send DAO
messages to inform their path on the network.

algorithm, which is used as a consistency indicator. In case of changes, this

Nowadays, performing tests and simulating protocol behaviour in industrial
scenarios are widely used as validation tools. In many cases, genuine hardware
usage is not viable due to cost, scalability, and infrastructure constraints. Ad-
ditionally, having a controlled environment for designing and testing networks
is often essential.
Therefore, wireless simulation has become a valuable tool for assisting in the
development and validation of new protocols and mechanisms. [ 65] identify four
use cases—Network Management Algorithms, Digital Factory, honeypots, and

Despite these limitations, simulation environments are a more feasible solution
in industrial contexts, offering a trade-off between accuracy and practical exper-
imentation.

2.5 Assessing TSCH Scheduling
TSCH scheduling determines industrial wireless networks’ performance, reliab-
ility, and resilience. As industrial IoT networks are subjected to diverse and
dynamic conditions, TSCH scheduling ensures that communication is organised
and interference is minimised. However, the effectiveness of TSCH scheduling
mechanisms requires careful evaluation, particularly in terms of how well they
manage network traﬀic and adapt to varying network loads. This subsection ex-
plores the key metrics used to assess TSCH scheduling, focussing on reliability,
latency, energy consumption, and network formation.
Analysing these metrics is essential to identifying opportunities for optimising
TSCH scheduling to support industrial IoT networks’ resilience and eﬀiciency.
In resilient industrial communication systems, the TSCH scheduling must dy-
namically adapt to both predictable and unexpected conditions, ensuring that
critical data is reliably transmitted with minimal delay. By examining these
metrics, this work aims to pinpoint specific areas where TSCH scheduling can
be refined, leading to robust, adaptable networks capable of meeting the strin-
gent demands of industrial applications.

2.5.1.2 Timing Metrics
In the manufacturing industry, timing requirements play a crucial role. For
example, Haerick and Gupta [ 34] discuss that process automation industries
typically require a cycle duration of 100 ms, which is included in class 4 NIST.
In comparison, factory automation industries have more stringent requirements
with a 10 ms cycle duration. Motion control applications have the strictest
timing requirements, with a 1 ms cycle time, 100 µs response time/update time,
and a redundancy switch-over time of less than 15 µs. Therefore, a monitoring
system plays an important role in such an environment. Despite data traversing
multiple technologies, each responsible for its resilience, measuring peer-to-peer
service metrics is essential to ensure the correct Quality of Service (QoS).
Like the previous group, one metric (Latency) holds more prominence than
the others. The overall latency value is calculated as the sum of delays until
the data reaches its endpoint. The following delays are general but should
be considered in TSCH: transmission, propagation, queuing, and processing.
It should be noted that the queueing delay is closely associated with TSCH,
representing the time that the data have spent waiting in the queue before being
sent. TSCH scheduling approaches significantly impact this delay, as allocating
cells or slotframe size by the 6top can influence how long the data remains in the
queue. First Data Packet Delivery (FDPD) was defined in [ 71] as the instant
the first data packet is received by the root node, for depicting the speed of the
installation of the SF.

chronise, join the RPL topology, and establish reliable communication. It en-
compasses the initial synchronisation phase, the association with the network
coordinator, and the construction of the routing topology through RPL. In this
context, the TSCH scheduler plays a critical role by determining how commu-
nication cells are allocated during the formation phase, directly impacting how
quickly nodes can exchange control messages and stabilise the network. This

4.2 Adaptive and Distributed Scheduling for TSCH

Each node in RPL mesh networks sends a node’s data (and passes it on to its

Defined by RFC 6206 [ 59], the Trickle Algorithm was designed to allow nodes
to exchange information in a shared medium. Even with the network’s stability
improving, it is possible to adjust transmission windows to reduce the number
of messages. As the network changes, the number of messages grows to distrib-

trickle timer to send DIO messages to disseminate information over the formed
topology. As cited by [ 44], RPL nodes use the trickle timer for sending DIO
messages, and the higher the trickle timer, the more stable the network. In addi-
tion, the work notes that the network stability relies on the interval between DIO
messages, as unstable networks require the transmission of more supplementary
DIO messages.
This stability-measuring instrument might be an essential indicator of network
health. Short trickle timers indicate topological changes in the neighbourhood.

RFC 6050 [ 58], a trickle timer is an integer value used as a base two-power

nodes. Without joining the DODAG, the node will not be able to generate or
send packets to other nodes, leading to interruptions in communication.

4.4 Discussion
This chapter introduces a novel TSCH scheduling approach based on the trickle
timer network consistency metric. It adheres to the minimum RFC 8180 beha-
viour but enhances it to mitigate shared-media issues such as channel occupa-
tion, retransmissions, and energy waste, which can lead to latency and data loss
at high data-sending rates. The proposed scheme maintains full compatibility
with RFC 8180, enabling coexistence with pure nodes.
Utilising the trickle timer metric optimises cell transmission negotiations
between nodes and their parents. It controls the intervals for broadcasting
DIO messages based on DODAG stability, resetting upon network topology
— 75 —

CHAPTER 4. DISTRIBUTED NETWORK-A W ARE TSCH SCHEDULING
disturbances triggered by DIS messages. This dynamic adjustment anticipates
topological changes, supports seamless node integration, and enhances network
resilience.
When the trickle timer indicates network stability, cells are deallocated, return-
ing nodes to basic operational configurations. This strategy improves packet de-
livery metrics and reduces retransmission efforts. Integrating metrics like queue
occupancy and RPL rank with the trickle timer assists in addressing bootstrap,
topological, and traﬀic challenges. Future plans include exploring additional
metrics for policy formulation and integrating slotframe length considerations
into scheduling algorithms.
A key innovation involves embedding TSCH cell allocation information in RPL
control messages, leveraging existing messaging to enhance traﬀic management
eﬀiciency across mesh networks. This method improves resource management
concerning latency, bandwidth, and energy consumption, aligning cell allocation
precisely with application needs.
The proposed mechanism enables proactive network preparation and poten-
tial performance improvements by anticipating traﬀic bursts from deep-location
nodes in dense DODAGs. Acknowledging limitations, such as the current over-
sight of network traﬀic patterns, future efforts will refine mechanisms to incor-
porate comprehensive traﬀic pattern analysis.
In conclusion, this approach significantly advances TSCH scheduling capabilit-
ies, promising enhanced performance and reliability in challenging network en-
vironments, which is particularly beneficial for industrial IoT applications.

performance, including increased eﬀiciency, controlled latency, and enhanced

herent challenges of TSCH scheduling, offering a scalable solution that adapts

The presented findings contribute valuable insights into the improvement of
the adaptability of the TSCH protocol, providing a framework for integrating
adaptive cross-layer strategies in industrial IoT networks. It lays the groundwork
for future research and development in robust and deterministic communication
technologies for industrial IoT settings.

requirements.

a more comprehensive range of industrial environments. Future studies should
explore the integration of hybrid deployments, incorporating both standard and
RippleTrickle nodes, to analyse the impact of the RT-ranked method in het-

proposed enhancements perform in real-world scenarios where various types of
node coexist.

significant potential to improve network adaptability. Predictive models could
be developed to anticipate network disturbances, allowing for proactively alloc-
ating TSCH cells, even in resource-constrained environments. Such an approach
could drastically enhance the network’s ability to adjust dynamically to varying
conditions.

study, which is another key area for future work. More advanced algorithms
